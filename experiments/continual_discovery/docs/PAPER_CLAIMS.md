## New simulator claims

Every new result generated by this pack must be labeled:

**Synthetic simulation / mechanism probe**

Recommended contribution framing:

> We extend the measured lineage-repair mechanism with a controlled synthetic question-acquisition probe. The simulation tests whether lineage-aware question selection acquires independent support more efficiently than random or confidence-only baselines under correlated evidence ancestry.

Do not write:

> Our 10,000-agent deployment demonstrates...

unless a real deployment experiment is separately executed.

## Claim-safe sentences for the runs in `results/`

Each of these is supported by a committed table and the config and seeds that
produced it. Keep the evidence-class label attached.

> In a synthetic mechanism probe over correlated evidence lineages (10 seeds,
> 2,000 claims per seed, populations labeled 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 with bounded
> per-claim routing), a combined continual-questioning policy reached 87.1-87.2%
> root-balanced accuracy against 82.6-83.4% for random, uncertainty-triggered,
> and lineage-only questioning, while spending about 1.9 questions per claim
> against random's 3.0.

> In the same simulation, lineage-preferring selection acquired about 0.93 new
> independent evidence roots per question against about 0.23 for random
> selection.

> A one-factor-at-a-time sensitivity sweep over root corruption rate, lineage
> concentration, question budget, the independent-root target, and candidate
> pool size (23 cells, 8 seeds each, seed-paired) found the accuracy advantage
> of the combined policy in all 23 cells.

Negative results that must travel with the claims above:

> The combined policy was worse than random questioning on false confident
> consensus in 15 of 23 sensitivity cells, most clearly at high root corruption
> (7.6% against 5.9% at `corrupt_root_prob = 0.30`): stopping early on a
> satisfied trigger leaves some confidently wrong claims that spending the full
> budget would have caught.

> Lineage-only questioning did not improve accuracy over random questioning
> (3 wins, 8 ties, 12 losses across the same cells). Its measured advantage is
> cost and calibration, not accuracy.

> The three population labels are not a scaling result. The per-claim candidate
> coalition is bounded, so all three columns describe the same workload.
