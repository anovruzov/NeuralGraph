Mycelic

In development

Spot quality problems that show up at several sites at once.

Each site checks its own records, a named person approves the follow-up, and raw records stay at the site.

About the pilot

How it works

  1. Each site counts. A small model on the site's own hardware reads complaint records and counts them by product, lot and failure mode.
  2. HQ looks across sites. It flags a failure mode that rises above its usual level at several sites, with no rule written in advance.
  3. Sites check. HQ asks each site to check its own records. Each answers yes, no or unknown, with a rough count. No complaint text leaves the site.
  4. A person approves. Mycelic drafts the follow-up, such as a CAPA initiation, for a named owner to approve. Every step is logged, and each site can trace its answer to its own records.

Why it runs inside your walls

Your complaint text may not be allowed to leave a site or a country. So the model runs on your own hardware, with no outside model service.

A central system cannot check records it is not allowed to hold. Each site can. If your complaint text already sits in one place, you may not need Mycelic.

Who it is for

Quality and safety teams at multi-site manufacturers, starting with medical devices.

Status and pilot

Each step is built and tested on synthetic records. In cloud test runs, small open models (0.5 to 4 billion parameters) on ordinary CPUs played six simulated sites: they read the synthetic complaints and answered HQ’s checks. Over five simulated worlds with 20 planted problems, 15 of them visible only in the complaint text, the two larger models’ reading found 9 and 10, the complaint codes alone 5, and simple word matching 19. When the same models read 150 real vehicle complaints with the codes hidden, our first attempt scored zero because our own post-processing discarded answers that named the vehicle. With that fixed, they matched the filed component codes far less often than simple word matching: 0.03, 0.18 and 0.22 against 0.43 on the same measure. On public complaint text, small models at the site do not yet beat a word list.

We also replayed two years of public complaints in two fields: FDA reports for one device manufacturer (eight recalls) and NHTSA complaints for six car makes (about 230 recalls, 896 recalled model-years). In both, the detectors flagged no recall earlier than chance would, and on the vehicle data they mostly flagged recalls after they were announced. Using the regulator's defect investigations instead of recalls gave the same answer. There are no customer results yet.

What does leave a site is weekly counts, and they say more than they look. In our own leakage test on synthetic data, someone who already held a particular complaint could tell from the counts whether it was in a site’s data and which failure it reported. The counts did not reveal people’s names. Complaint text never left a site in our scans for planted text. One large scan did report a hit, and we traced it to our own scanner: a planted marker happened to match part of a site’s name. With that fixed, the scan passes in our offline re-runs; the re-run with a small model in the loop comes next.

The code for these steps is public on a development branch of the repository, not yet on its main branch.

The pilot is a 4 to 6 week signal audit for design partners. It runs on your historical complaint and CAPA exports, inside your environment, before any deployment. We compare what it flags with your past CAPAs, and list what it flagged that no CAPA covers. It writes nothing to your quality system. The audit is public code that runs on a laptop with nothing installed but Python, so your team can try it on an export before any call: how to run the audit.
