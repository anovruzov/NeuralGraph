## 2. The three claims

### Claim 1 — replica count does not buy survival

Three replicas of a premise score **0.00** against a lineage-root failure.
Full replication at eight records — twice any other strategy's storage — also
scores **0.00**. Every copy descends from the root that failed.

This holds at every scale tested: 0 of 16 capability shapes (K ∈ {2,3,5,8},
H ∈ {2,3}) survive, under both repair policies.

### Claim 2 — lineage-aware repair is Pareto-better than replication

**1.00** on the same failure, at **half the storage** (4 records vs 8) and
**fewer bytes moved** (5448 vs 9877). Higher survival at lower cost on both
axes.

### Claim 3 — minimum failure-domain cut predicts survival

`worst_single_domain_failure` fails exactly one domain — never a whole cut,
which would be tautological. Only the min-cut-2 placement survives it; every
min-cut-1 strategy scores 0.00.

The metric is therefore **predictive**, not descriptive.

## 4. Results reported against interest

These weaken the headline. They are pinned as tests so they cannot be dropped.

### Distribution loses to centralization under partition

**No distributed strategy** survives a network partition that isolates a single
premise holder — 0.00 across all five. Only `centralized` and
`full_replication` survive, because they keep every premise on one node.

This is the real cost of distribution, and it is not small.

### Lineage-aware repair does not dominate

Under a **plain node failure with correlated replicas**, a surviving replica
would have served — the lineage was never the problem, the node was. But
lineage-aware repair excludes the failed claim's root and refuses every
correlated holder:

| | survival |
|---|---|
| `source_count` repair | **1.00** |
| `lineage_aware` repair | **0.00** |

Treating correlation as disqualifying regardless of what actually failed has a
real cost. Where independent support exists, the cost vanishes and
lineage-awareness is also cheaper (5.2 vs 6.2 verifications).

### Centralization fails silently

`centralized` has the worst unrecovered silent forgetting of any strategy
(0.667): it loses the capability while still reporting confidence it has not
earned.

### The random baseline agrees with theory

`random_path_diversification` lands at **0.67** against a closed-form **2/3** —
the odds of placing the independent node in the first two of three candidates.
Agreement with the analytic value is the check that the harness measures the
mechanism it claims to, rather than an artefact.

## 5. Real-storage validation

The benchmark runs on mocks. These results are reproduced against four real
`SQLiteNeuralGraphStorage` files, one per node, through the real
`NeuralGraphMemoryAdapter` and `StorageLineageResolver`, with lineage **derived**
from stored HIERARCHY edges rather than declared by the fixture:

- reconstruction from premises no single store holds
- replica count failing to survive its shared root
- the silent-forgetting signature — confidence above genuine coverage
- independent lineage restoring the capability
- verification separating apparent from genuine independence
- payload-free denials
- survival across closing and reopening every store
- **min-cut 1** for the standard placement and **min-cut 2** for the oracle

Correlated replicas in this fixture share **no rows** and live in **separate
database files**. What makes them correlated is a shared upstream ancestor,
which is how correlated provenance actually appears in deployment.

## 6. Harness bugs found before these numbers were trusted

Each would have produced a publishable-looking but wrong table. Recorded
because "the benchmark ran" is not the same as "the benchmark measured
something".

| Bug | Effect if undetected |
|---|---|
| `max_nodes` truncated the route | The independently-rooted node was never contacted, making lineage diversity invisible to every strategy at once |
| Repair read `Placement` internals | Policies were oracles, not policies |
| Lineage-awareness excluded but never *ordered* | Budget exhausted on correlated nodes; **random appeared to beat lineage-aware** |
| `expected_answer` hardcoded to two slots | Every K>2 baseline failed; whole sweep read 0.00 |
| Sorted-by-name route preference | Never reached premise K−1 |
| Slot relevance ranked ahead of policy principle | Known-compromised holders ranked first — reintroduced the ordering bug |
| Verification budget fixed at 2 while pool grew as K·(H−1) | A decline would have measured the budget, not the mechanism |

Also rejected as **tautological**: failing an entire min-cut. A cut severs every
coalition by definition, so every strategy scores zero and the cell carries no
information. Replaced with a single worst-domain failure, which discriminates.

## 7. What is not shown

- **Local retrieval accuracy is untouched.** The 50% single-hop / 77% recall@50
  figure in `QUERY_ROUTING_IMPROVEMENTS.md` has no committed artifact behind it,
  and the QA benchmarks need a live Ollama at `localhost:11434`, so they are not
  reproducible from a clean checkout. This belongs to the NeuralGraph track.
- **No wall-clock latency**, anywhere, deliberately. A mock adapter's timings
  would say nothing about a deployed system.
- **No transport.** NATS/JetStream is design intent; everything runs in-process.
- **Failure domains must be recorded** by a deployment to be measured. Absent
  recording, domain metrics stay visibly zero rather than being synthesized.
