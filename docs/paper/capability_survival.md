## Scope, stated first

This paper reports a **simulation study of lineage-aware placement and repair**
under controlled failure. It is deliberately narrow.

**What is measured.** Capability survival across 8 placement/repair strategies
× 9 interventions × 30 seeds, on an in-process coordination layer, with the
headline result reproduced against real SQLite storage.

**What is not implemented, and is not claimed.** NATS/JetStream transport is
design intent; **every experiment runs in a single process**. There is no
wall-clock latency measurement anywhere, deliberately — a mock adapter's
timings would say nothing about a deployed system. There is no organizational
abstraction hierarchy, no cross-cutting scope hyperedge, no learned router, and
no hyperbolic embedding. These are listed in [Future work](#9-future-work-not-implemented)
and are marked *proposed* wherever they appear.

**A naming caution.** `NeuralGraph/tesseract.py` is **intra-node retrieval
fusion**. It is not a distributed router, and nothing in this paper depends on
it. Separately, the eight *strategies* evaluated here vary where records are
placed and how repair chooses candidates. They are **not** eight memory
architectures, and this paper makes no centralized-vs-federated architecture
comparison.

## 1. Introduction

A collective of agents, each holding part of what is needed to answer a
question, can reconstruct answers no single member holds. This paper is about
how that capability is lost.

It can be lost quietly. Every node reports healthy, every replica is intact,
storage-level redundancy metrics look fine — and the collective can no longer
answer. We call this **collective forgetting**: the capability is gone while
every local signal still reports health.

The central negative result is that **replica count does not protect against
it**. Copies that share a derivation ancestor fail together. Eight replicas
descending from one root are, against a failure of that root, worth exactly
one. What protects capability is **lineage independence**, and the quantity
that predicts survival is the **minimum failure-domain cut**.

### Contributions

1. **Collective forgetting** defined and measured as a distinct failure mode:
   capability lost while confidence stays high.
2. **Lineage-aware repair**, which reaches higher survival than full
   replication at half the storage and fewer bytes moved.
3. **Minimum failure-domain cut** validated as a predictor of survival, against
   a non-tautological intervention.
4. An **8 × 9 × 30-seed** evaluation with matched budgets, an oracle upper
   bound, and every harness bug that changed a conclusion recorded.
5. The headline reproduced on **real SQLite** with lineage *derived* from the
   stored graph rather than declared.
6. **Scale and arity generalization**: every verdict invariant across
   K ∈ {2,3,5,8}, H ∈ {2,3}; reconstruction correct for arity 2–8.
7. **Negative results reported against interest**, including two where the
   proposed method loses.

## 2. Model

Agent *i* owns a local memory graph *G_i*. There are no raw cross-graph edges;
exchange happens through typed contracts (`coordination/contracts.py`).

A **capability** is a proposition *k* requiring evidence from *H* premise slots.
The collective *has* *k* if some authorized coalition of nodes can jointly
supply every slot. **Derivation parents** are the targets of outgoing HIERARCHY
edges plus `source_memory_ids`; direction is load-bearing, and `parent_id` and
`get_edges_to` point the wrong way.

A **failure domain** is the upstream origin a memory ultimately derives from,
read from the metadata of the lineage **roots** the walk resolves — never from
the retrieved node. This derivation is **ingestion provenance**, and stating
which derivation was used is not optional: inferring a domain from storage
identity, node id, or file path would make domain diversity a restatement of
node diversity and collapse min-cut toward replica count.

Let *P_k* be the authorized derivation paths reconstructing *k*, each depending
on a set of domains *D(p)*. The survivability margin is the minimum-cost set of
failures hitting every path:

> κ(k) = min { c(F) : ∀p ∈ P_k, D(p) ∩ F ≠ ∅ }

This is recognizably a hitting-set / min-cut object. **The novelty is not the
formalism.** It is the demonstration that correlation *through derivation* —
not independent component failure — is the binding constraint on capability
survival, and that a lineage-aware policy exploits this at lower cost than
replication.

**Safety property.** Partial provenance is *pessimistic*. An unrecorded domain
shrinks the reported cut and can never inflate it, so a partially instrumented
deployment under-claims robustness. This is what makes the metric publishable
from incomplete data, and it is asserted as a test
(`test_partial_provenance_is_pessimistic_never_optimistic`), not argued.

## 3. Experimental design

**8 strategies × 9 interventions × 30 seeds = 72 cells**, matched budgets.

Strategies span `isolated_local` (no coordination) through `centralized`,
`full_replication` (2× the storage of any other), three repair policies, and
`oracle_min_cut` as an upper bound. Interventions span node failure, memory
deletion, lineage-root failure, authorization revocation, route removal, edge
corruption, stale knowledge, network partition, and worst single-domain failure.

Three design decisions are load-bearing:

**Repair policies see exported contracts only.** A policy that reads
`Placement` internals or node-private records is an oracle, not a policy. This
was a bug once; it is now a test.

**The routing bound is kept and documented.** Lifting it entirely collapses the
experiment the other way — repair never fires and every policy ties at 0.88.
Both settings are reported.

**Failing an entire min-cut was rejected as tautological.** A cut severs every
coalition by definition, so every strategy scores zero and the cell carries no
information. It is replaced by `worst_single_domain_failure`, which fails
exactly **one** domain and discriminates.

## 4. Results

See [`tables.md`](tables.md) for all five generated tables.

### 4.1 Headline

`lineage_aware_repair` reaches **0.778** survival at 4 records and 5,448 mean
bytes moved. `full_replication` reaches **0.667** at 8 records and 9,877 bytes
— **higher survival at half the storage and 45% fewer bytes**. `oracle_min_cut`
bounds the achievable at **0.889**.

`centralized` scores 0.111 survival but **0.667 unrecovered silent forgetting**,
the worst on that axis: it fails quietly.

### 4.2 Replica count does not buy survival

Against `lineage_root_failure`: `full_replication` **0.00 (0/30)**,
`fixed_distributed_replication` **0.00**, `source_count_repair` **0.00**.
Three replicas and eight replicas are equally worthless when every copy
descends from the root that failed. `lineage_aware_repair` scores **1.00
(30/30)** on the same failure.

This holds at every scale tested: 0 of 16 capability shapes survive, under both
repair policies (`replication_effect_under_correlated_lineage`, all cells 0.00).

### 4.3 Min-cut predicts survival

Under `worst_single_domain_failure` — one domain, never a whole cut — **only
`oracle_min_cut` survives (1.00, 30/30)**. It is the only placement with a
minimum failure-domain cut of 2. Every min-cut-1 placement scores 0.00,
including `lineage_aware_repair`.

This is a **validation, not a demonstration**: the oracle ties lineage-aware
repair on seven of nine interventions and separates only on the one designed to
test min-cut.

### 4.4 Independence, not scale, is the controlling variable

Every one of the 12 policy × intervention × independence cells is **invariant
across scale** (K ∈ {2,3,5,8}, H ∈ {2,3}). What changes survival is the number
of **independent roots**: at I=1 every policy fails lineage-root failure and
authorization revocation; at I=2 every policy survives them.

### 4.5 Real storage

The headline is reproduced against four real SQLite databases with lineage
**derived from the stored graph**, not declared: min-cut 1 for the standard
placement, **min-cut 2 for the oracle**, and no single domain severs every
coalition. Results survive closing and reopening every store.

## 5. Results reported against interest

**N1 — distribution loses to centralization under partition.** No distributed
strategy survives a partition isolating a single premise holder: **0.00 across
all six**. Only `centralized` and `full_replication` survive, because they keep
every premise on one node. That is the real cost of distribution, and it is
pinned as a test.

**N2 — lineage-aware repair does not dominate.** It loses to `source_count` in
exactly **one of six** policy × intervention cells:
`lineage_aware::node_failure::I1`, scoring **0.00 against 1.00**. With a single
independent root there is no independent holder to find, so refusing every
correlated holder discards a replica that would have served. The node was the
problem; the lineage never was.

*(This is narrower than earlier drafts stated. At I=2 the two policies tie at
1.00. The controlling variable is independent roots, not K or H — see
`AUDIT.md` §2.)*

**N3 — domain metrics require recorded provenance.** Failure domains are empty
by default and never synthesized. A deployment that does not record them sees
zeros, not inferred values.

**N5 — the stochastic baseline matches its closed form.** 
`random_path_diversification` lands at **0.667 (20/30)** against a closed-form
2/3 — the odds of placing the independent node in the first two of three
candidates. This agreement is the check that the harness measures the mechanism
it claims to.

### Harness bugs that changed conclusions

Seven were found before any of this was trusted. Three changed results:
`max_nodes` truncated the route so the independently-rooted node was never
contacted, making lineage diversity invisible to every strategy at once; repair
policies ranked candidates by reading `Placement` internals; and lineage
awareness acted as a verification-time *exclusion* but never as an *ordering*,
so a bounded budget was exhausted on correlated nodes — under which
`random_path_diversification` **appeared to beat** `lineage_aware_repair`. The
full list belongs in an appendix, not hidden.

## 7. Limitations

- **No transport.** Everything is in-process. NATS/JetStream is design intent.
- **No wall-clock latency**, deliberately.
- **Simulation plus SQLite only.** No deployed multi-machine system.
- **Failure domains must be recorded** to be measured; absent recording they
  stay visibly zero.
- **Local retrieval accuracy is out of scope** and untouched. It is diagnosed
  in `docs/ACCURACY.md` (single-hop 52.1%, a generation rather than retrieval
  problem, hard ceiling 96.9%) and **nothing has been fixed**. No LoCoMo score
  is claimed here.

## 8. Related work

Erasure coding and replica placement optimize for *independent* component
failure; the result here is that correlation through **derivation** is the
binding constraint, which replica-count metrics cannot see. Lineage-driven
fault injection (Alvaro et al., SIGMOD 2015) reasons backward from outcomes to
causally relevant failures, and is the closest prior art in spirit. Provenance
systems and multi-agent memory governance supply the derivation-typing
vocabulary. This paper claims neither graph memory, nor persistent memory, nor
min-cut as standalone novelty.

## 9. Future work (not implemented)

Every item below is **proposed** and has **no implementation, test, or
artifact** in this repository. None is claimed as a result.

- **Durable transport.** NATS/JetStream for replay and disconnected recovery.
- **Organizational abstraction hierarchy** across agent/team/department levels
  with reversible lineage drill-down.
- **Cross-cutting scopes** (project, mission, incident) as overlapping
  hyperedges rather than tree nodes.
- **Learned set-valued routing** fusing semantic, scope, temporal, lineage,
  policy and cost signals.
- **Hyperbolic representation** for the hierarchical component of scope, to be
  settled by ablation rather than assumed.
- **Active lineage diversification**: a controller that detects high-value
  claims with low κ(k) and acquires a disjoint verification path. The verifier
  must not see the existing answer, or it produces correlated confirmation
  rather than independent support.
- **Matched architecture comparison** across centralized, federated, and
  flat-distributed families. **None of these families is implemented**; the
  eight strategies evaluated here are placement/repair policies, not
  architectures.

---

## 10. Conclusion

Measure lineage diversity, not replica count. A collective can hold eight
copies of a premise and lose the capability the moment their shared ancestor
fails, while every local health signal stays green. The minimum failure-domain
cut predicts that loss; a repair policy that orders candidates by lineage
independence exploits it at half the storage of full replication. It does not
dominate — under a plain node failure with a single independent root, a
correlated replica would have served, and refusing it costs a capability. Both
directions are reported.
